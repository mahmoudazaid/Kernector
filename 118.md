
# Technical Mock Interview

The technical mock interview is the final hurdle in the Career Module. Unlike the HR interview, this session is a deep dive into your technical fluency, problem-solving logic, and ability to communicate complex ideas to a technical mentor.

## Overview

This interview is conducted by a Senior Team Lead (STL) and is based on the two job postings you provide. It's designed to check market readiness, which means it may be more challenging than some real interviews. That's intentional.

The STL will act as the hiring manager or technical lead. They aren't just looking for the right answer; they're evaluating your technical maturity, how you handle edge cases, how you explain your reasoning, and how you admit to gaps in your knowledge.

### Technical Mock Interview: Preparation Guide

> ⚠️ This is a one-time opportunity. If you go into the mock interview without revising the relevant material, the feedback will reflect your level of preparation, not your actual potential.
>
> Prepare in advance to make the most of the experience.
> Estimated minimum preparation time: one full day.

Before you book, choose two job postings for roles you'd realistically consider applying for. The interview will be adapted to fit those specific roles. Upload screenshots of both postings as your project when you book.

Along with the two job postings, you'll also need to submit the project you'll use for the technical deep-dive: your capstone if it's done, or your strongest available project otherwise. This is what the reviewer will dig into during the interview, so make sure it's the one you feel most confident presenting and defending.

**How to prepare technically:**

Go through the requirements in your chosen postings carefully. If a role requires SQL, work through SQL exercises before the interview. If it's a product analyst role, prepare for A/B testing questions. Treat the job posting as the syllabus.

For concrete questions to practise, check the "Sample questions" section in each of your Turing College graded project descriptions. Searching for "[role] interview questions" is also a reliable way to find what interviewers ask, because recruiters often do the same search when preparing.

**What the interview tests beyond technical knowledge:**

It's a significant mistake to think your soft skills aren't being evaluated in a technical interview. You'll often be speaking directly with the person you'd work under if hired. They're deciding whether they'd enjoy working with you, not just whether you can answer technical questions. If they feel it would be difficult or unpleasant to work with you, technical accuracy won't be enough.

![Stand out in Tech Interview](https://intra-content-image-bucket.s3.eu-north-1.amazonaws.com/images/1791469460360-shcmwko1ow.webp)

At minimum: be friendly, curious, and polite. Beyond that:

- **Think through real use cases, not just theory.** When answering, demonstrate that you can apply a concept, not just define it. If asked about A/B testing, discuss practical considerations: how you'd separate groups, how long the test would need to run given the traffic available. This is what distinguishes someone who has read about something from someone who can implement it.
- **Connect answers to your actual experience.** Reference your TC projects where relevant. If asked about a concept you covered in a graded task, say so and describe how you encountered it. Every company asks questions like this.
- **Be deliberate about what you mention.** Don't bring up topics you're not confident in, or be upfront that you're not entirely sure. Interviewers will dig into whatever you raise. You can also use this the other way: mention topics you're strong in, and the interviewer may follow your lead.
- **Pauses are fine.** If you need a moment to think, take it. Up to around 10–15 seconds is normal. After that, start talking through your thinking aloud. Even an incomplete answer that shows clear reasoning is better than silence or bluffing.
- **Don't Google.** In a remote interview, it's obvious. It disqualifies candidates immediately.
- **If you don't know something, say so clearly and explain how you'd find the answer.** Confidently saying "I don't know, but here's how I'd approach it" is more impressive than a hesitant or fabricated answer.
- **When the interviewer explains something you got wrong, show you understood it.** Ask a follow-up question or connect it to something you do know. This signals how easy you'd be to work with and teach.

> 📌 You won't be asked to complete coding tasks during the technical mock interview. The interview is conversational, based on your chosen job postings.

> ⚠️ **The no-Google rule:** During the interview, searching for answers is not allowed. If you don't know an answer, walk the interviewer through how you would find it or the logic you would use to solve it.

## Schedule Your Session

When you're ready, upload your two job posting screenshots along with your capstone (or your strongest project) as your project submission, and book your session directly in the platform.

> **Note:** Treat this exactly like a real job interview. Ensure you have a stable internet connection, a quiet environment, and are prepared to join the call on time. If you need to cancel or reschedule, please do so at least 24 hours in advance.
